How to brew pour-over coffee

A clean, sweet cup in three minutes, with a recipe you can repeat every morning.

  • Total time 3 min
  • Difficulty: easy-ish
  • Makes 1 cup (about 230 ml)

Why pour-over

Pour-over is the brewer for people who want to taste the coffee itself. Hot water runs through a bed of grounds and a paper filter, which holds back most of the oils and fine particles. What reaches the cup is light-bodied and clear, so the fruit in a washed Chikmagalur or the jasmine in an Ethiopian coffee comes through. It asks for a little attention, mostly a slow, steady pour. Once you've made it three or four times, it becomes a calm two-and-a-half-minute habit.

You'll need

  • A cone dripper (any size 01 or 02 cone works)
  • A paper filter to fit it
  • A kettle, ideally a gooseneck for a thin, controllable stream
  • A digital scale that reads in grams
  • A timer (your phone is fine)
  • A mug or server
  • A grinder, optional: if you don't have one, choose Pour-over as your grind and we'll do it for you

No. 02 · Steps

Steps

  1. 1

    Before you start

    Step 1: Heat the water.

    Bring fresh water to the boil, then let it sit for about 30 seconds so it settles at 92–94 °C.

    Why: water that's too hot pulls out bitterness; water that's too cool leaves the cup thin and sour.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Rinse the filter.

    Set the filter in the cone, place it on your mug, and pour hot water through until the paper is soaked. Tip that water away.

    Why: it washes out the papery taste and warms the cone and mug so the brew doesn't cool too fast.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Add the coffee.

    Put the cone and mug on the scale. Add 15 g of medium-fine coffee, shake the cone gently so the bed is flat, and tare the scale to zero.

    Why: a level bed lets water flow evenly instead of finding one channel through the middle.

  4. 4

    0:00

    Step 4: Bloom.

    Start the timer and pour 30 g of water, about twice the weight of the coffee, in a slow spiral until every ground is wet. Wait until 0:40.

    Why: fresh coffee holds carbon dioxide from roasting. The bloom lets that gas escape, so the water can reach the coffee properly. You'll see it puff up and bubble. That's a sign of freshness.

    that puff and bubble is freshness showing

  5. 5

    0:40

    Step 5: First pour.

    Pour in slow circles from the centre outwards, avoiding the very edge of the filter, until the scale reads 150 g.

    Why: pouring on the paper's edge lets water bypass the coffee and dilutes the cup.

  6. 6

    1:15

    Step 6: Second pour.

    Once the water level has dropped a little, pour again in the same way up to 250 g.

    Why: two pours keep the temperature and the bed height steady, which makes the brew more even than one big pour.

  7. 7

    1:45

    Step 7: Swirl and let it drain.

    Give the cone one gentle swirl to flatten the bed. Let it drip through. It should finish between 2:30 and 3:00.

    Why: a flat bed at the end means every ground got the same treatment. The finish time is your best guide to the grind.

  8. 8

    2:30–3:00

    Step 8: Serve.

    Remove the cone, give the coffee a swirl, and let it cool for a minute or two. Pour-over tastes sweeter as it drops from scalding to hot.

No. 03 · Fixes

If it doesn't taste right

Taste problems, likely causes and fixes
It tastes…Likely causeTry this
Sour, sharp, thinUnder-extracted: water passed through too fastGrind a little finer, or use water at 94 °C
Bitter, dry, harshOver-extracted: water stayed too longGrind a little coarser, or finish the pours sooner
Weak but not sourNot enough coffee for the waterUse 16 g instead of 15 g
Drains in under 2:00Grind too coarseGrind finer
Still dripping after 3:30Grind too fine, or the bed is cloggedGrind coarser and pour more gently

Change one thing at a time, so you know what fixed it.

Two ways to change it up

  1. Iced pour-over.

    Put 100 g of ice in the server. Brew with 15 g of coffee and 150 g of hot water, ground a touch finer. The coffee melts the ice as it drips, so it's chilled and at full strength. Fruity naturals are great here.

  2. Bigger batch for two.

    Double everything: 30 g coffee, 500 g water, a 60 g bloom. Expect a total time closer to 3:30–4:00, and grind slightly coarser to keep it from turning bitter.
