Meta Ads  /  campaign analysis

Spend climbing, purchases flat

Challenge

Budget rising month over month with no matching movement in purchases, and no obvious culprit.

Analysis

Spend broken down by campaign, ad set, audience and creative, then delivery compared before and after the last bid strategy change.

Insight

Two broad-audience ad sets absorbed most of the incremental budget at roughly double the account's blended cost per purchase.

Action

Cap the two ad sets, move budget to the three converting at target, refresh creative on the fatigued set before scaling again.

Google Ads  /  campaign analysis

A quarter under review before renewal

Challenge

Search spend needing defence ahead of a budget renewal conversation with the client.

Analysis

Campaign efficiency compared by match type, device and query intent, with platform conversions reconciled against analytics.

Insight

Broad match was absorbing budget on informational queries, and platform-reported conversions double-counted returning users.

Action

Tighten match types, add the negative list, and report the reconciled figure so the client sees one consistent number.

GA4  /  conversion analysis

Carts filling, checkouts empty

Challenge

Healthy paid traffic and add-to-carts, but completed purchases well below the category norm.

Analysis

Funnel mapped step by step, split by device and traffic source, with event firing checked at every stage.

Insight

Mobile users dropped disproportionately at the shipping step, and one checkout event was not firing at all — understating conversions.

Action

Fix the tracking first so the baseline is real, then address the mobile shipping step before spending more on traffic.

Agency workflow  /  automation

Month-end taking days across the team

Challenge

Every account manager reporting differently, with the close of each month consuming several days of senior time.

Analysis

Documented what each report contained, which metrics were defined inconsistently, and where the manual hours went.

Insight

Most of the effort was collection and formatting. The part clients valued was the smallest share of the time.

Action

Automate collection and formatting to one standard template, and redirect the recovered hours into commentary and recommendations.

Asked first

Nine questions agencies open with

Do you work directly with our clients?

No. Every conversation, deliverable and decision goes through your team. We never contact your clients, and we don't need to know who they are.

Will our clients know we work with an outside analyst?

Only if you tell them. Everything ships under your agency's brand, and nothing we produce carries our name unless you ask for it.

What platforms do you support?

Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4, Google Search Console, Looker Studio and Google Sheets as standard. Other sources — CRMs, ecommerce platforms, call tracking — are reviewed case by case.

Can you use our branding?

Yes. Your template, fonts, colors and logo, or fully unbranded if you would rather place the work into your own document yourself.

How often do you report?

Most agencies run a monthly cycle. Quarterly and custom cadences work equally well, and deadlines are set to land before your client meeting.

Do you provide recommendations, or only reports?

Both. A report without a recommendation leaves the hardest part with your account manager, so every cycle ends with ranked actions and the reasoning behind them.

Can you handle multiple clients at once?

Yes — one standard format applied across your roster is usually where the biggest time saving comes from.

How quickly can we start?

Usually one call and a permissions email. Once access is granted, the first cycle can begin immediately.

What if we only need help occasionally?

One-off work is welcome. A single campaign audit, funnel analysis or dashboard build doesn't need an ongoing arrangement — tell us the scope and you'll get it back as a defined piece of work.
