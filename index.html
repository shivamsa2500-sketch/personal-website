01The problem

Your team shouldn't spend Friday building reports.

Reporting is the work that grows fastest with your client count and adds the least to your margin. It's also the first thing that slips when the month gets busy.

01Exporting from four platforms

Google Ads, Meta, GA4 and Search Console, each with its own date logic and its own definition of a conversion.

02Cleaning the same spreadsheet

Mismatched campaign names, duplicate rows and columns that break the moment someone renames an ad set.

03Rebuilding charts monthly

The same slides remade from scratch every cycle because nothing was built to refresh.

04Writing the same commentary

Account managers explaining performance in their own words, differently for every client.

05Senior time on repetitive work

The people who should be planning next quarter are formatting tables instead.

06No analyst to hand it to

A full-time analyst is a serious salary commitment for work that peaks a few days a month.

So hand the whole cycle over.

We collect the data, analyze it, build the report and write the recommendations — delivered in your template, ready for your client meeting. Your team reviews it instead of producing it.

02Services

An outsourced analytics department.

Six services covering the analytical work an agency either does badly under time pressure or turns down entirely.

White-label reporting

Monthly client-ready reports delivered under your agency's brand — your template, your fonts, your logo, or unbranded if you prefer to place it yourself.

  • Monthly, quarterly or custom cycles
  • Commentary an account manager can present
  • One consistent format across your roster
  • Delivered before the client meeting

Marketing dashboards

One live view combining every platform a client runs, so nobody opens four tabs to answer a question mid-call.

  • Google Ads, Meta, GA4 and Search Console together
  • CRM and sheet sources where they exist
  • Separate agency and client-facing views
  • Refreshes on schedule, no manual rebuilds

Campaign analysis

The part past reporting the numbers: which campaigns lose money, why the CPA moved, and what to change before next month's spend.

  • Inefficient campaigns and budget misallocation
  • CPA and ROAS movement, explained not noted
  • Audience, placement and creative performance
  • A ranked list of optimization opportunities

Conversion & funnel analysis

Where paid traffic stops converting once it lands, and which fix is worth doing first based on the revenue behind it.

  • Landing page and site journey analysis
  • Form, lead-flow and checkout drop-off mapping
  • Conversion rate by source, device and segment
  • Tracking audits where the data looks wrong

Analytics automation

The chain built once and left running: collection, processing, dashboard, report, delivery. The monthly cycle stops depending on who has time.

  • Scheduled pulls from ad platforms and analytics
  • Cleaning and consolidation into one dataset
  • Automated report generation and delivery
  • Alerts when a metric breaks a threshold

Data consolidation

For agencies sitting on years of inconsistent exports — one cleaned, documented dataset with an automated refresh going forward.

  • Historic export cleanup and deduplication
  • Standardized metric and campaign naming
  • Platform vs. analytics reconciliation
  • A single source your reports build on

05Demonstration projects

How the analysis actually runs.

Worked examples on representative data, showing the reasoning end to end. These are demonstrations, not client case studies — no client results are published here without written permission.

meta_ads
Campaign analysisDemonstration

Meta Ads performance analysis

Challenge
Spend climbing month over month while purchases stayed flat, with no clear culprit.
Analysis
Broke spend down by campaign, ad set, audience and creative, then compared delivery before and after the last bid strategy change.
Insight
Two broad-audience ad sets absorbed most of the incremental budget at roughly double the account's blended cost per purchase.
Action
Cap the two ad sets, move budget to the three converting at target, and refresh creative on the fatigued set before scaling again.
google_ads
Campaign analysisDemonstration

Google Ads campaign analysis

Challenge
A quarter of search spend under review ahead of a budget renewal conversation.
Analysis
Compared campaign efficiency by match type, device and query intent, and reconciled platform conversions against GA4.
Insight
Broad match was absorbing budget on informational queries, and platform-reported conversions were double-counting returning users.
Action
Tighten match types, add the negative list, and report on the reconciled figure so the client sees one consistent number.
ga4_funnel
Conversion analysisDemonstration

GA4 conversion analysis

Challenge
Healthy paid traffic and add-to-carts, but completed purchases well below the category norm.
Analysis
Mapped the funnel step by step in GA4, split by device and traffic source, and checked event firing at each stage.
Insight
Mobile users dropped disproportionately at the shipping step, and one checkout event wasn't firing at all — understating conversions.
Action
Fix the tracking first so the baseline is real, then address the mobile shipping step before spending more on traffic.

Monthly agency reporting workflow

Challenge
Every account manager reporting differently, with month-end consuming several days across the team.
Analysis

Insight
Most of the effort was collection and formatting, not analysis — the part clients valued was the smallest share of the time.
Action
Automate collection and formatting to one standard template, and redirect the recovered hours into commentary and recommendations.

09FAQ

The questions agencies ask first.

Do you work directly with our clients?

No. We operate entirely behind your agency. Your client keeps one point of contact — you. We don't email them, join their calls or appear on their invoices, and we never approach them independently.

Will our clients know Analyst Solution is involved?

Only if you choose to tell them. Everything is delivered in your template and branding, or completely unbranded so you can place it yourself. Nothing we produce carries our name unless you ask it to.

What platforms do you support?

Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4, Google Search Console, Looker Studio and Google Sheets as standard. CRM exports and other data sources are reviewed case by case before we commit to them — we'll tell you honestly if something isn't a fit.

Can you use our branding?

Yes. Send your template, logo, fonts and colors during onboarding and reports arrive matching them. If you don't have a template, we'll build one to your specification and it becomes your standard across every client.

How often do you report?

Monthly is standard, because it matches most agency retainer cycles. Weekly is available for high-spend accounts, and dashboards refresh continuously regardless of the reporting cadence. Custom schedules are fine — tell us when your client meetings fall and delivery lands before them.

Do you provide recommendations, or only reports?

Recommendations are the point. Every deliverable states what changed, why it changed, and what should happen next, ranked by expected impact. A report that only shows numbers is something your reporting tool already does.

Can you handle multiple clients at once?

Yes, and it gets more efficient as the count rises. Once one reporting standard is built for your agency, adding a client is a configuration step rather than a new build. Pricing reflects that.

How quickly can we start?

A single audit or analysis can usually begin within a few days of access being granted. A full reporting setup takes longer for the first cycle, since the template and data connections are built once — after that it runs to schedule. You'll get a specific timeline with your quote rather than a general promise.

What if we only need help occasionally?

That's a project engagement rather than a retainer. A campaign audit, a funnel analysis or a one-off dashboard build is quoted as a fixed price for the scope, with no ongoing commitment.
